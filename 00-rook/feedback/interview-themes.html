Dispatch interview themes

Summary of recurring issues from responder and handler interviews across Dispatch and related product feedback.

Theme 1

Callout timing feels unreliable

  • Responders reported both offers arriving too quickly and periods that feel too quiet.
  • Some said the phone buzzed and the callout was already gone; others said nothing arrived even when they were available.
  • That creates a perception that Dispatch is not consistently hitting the right moment.
Theme 2

The console is not glanceable enough

  • Handlers mentioned small text, hard-to-read status badges, and poor contrast.
  • Several people asked for larger text and clearer state differentiation.
  • For a console used while multitasking, this affects speed and operational confidence.
Theme 3

The system does not explain enough of what is happening

  • Handlers described seeing mismatched coverage cards or quiet periods without context.
  • The issue was not only wrong behavior; it was poor explainability.
  • People wanted to understand why one responder was skipped, offered, or left idle.
Theme 4

Filters and state persistence are trust-sensitive

  • Even small state-reset problems were treated as meaningful because they affect trust in the console.
  • Handlers wanted a reliable view from the start of a shift, not repeated housekeeping.
  • These small issues can sharply reduce confidence in the overall experience.
Theme 5

“Small” UX issues are operationally important

  • Examples included badge legibility, dark mode for overnight work, alert clarity, and filter trust.
  • These were described as minor, but they matter when the user is working under pressure.
  • Operational trust is fragile, and small UI failures feel much larger in live callouts.

Overall read

The interviews suggest a common issue: people do not just need better ranking; they need more confidence in the system’s live behavior. The product may be technically functional, but the user experience is undermining trust because the console and the callout flow are not transparent, consistent, or readable enough under real operational conditions.

Follow-up questions

  • Are the “too fast” and “too quiet” complaints coming from the same underlying mechanism, or are they distinct failure modes?
  • Is the core problem in ranking, offer delivery, or the clarity of the handler’s console state?
  • Which concerns are immediate product risks versus longer-term quality-of-life asks?
  • Are there metrics that can separate seasonal effects from actual product behavior changes?
