03 · The mechanics

04 / 07

The code has no automatic
way back into the queue.

A responder is not removed for being quiet. But after declines or timeouts lower the acceptance score, the code never restores that score just because time passed.

1

Available responders

Everyone marked available stays in the ranking list.

2

Ranking order

Location, acceptance history, and capability decide who is asked first.

3

60 seconds to answer

Accept: stop. Decline or timeout: move to the next responder.

4

Score changes

Accept adds points. Decline and timeout both subtract points.

5

No time recovery

If ranking stops reaching someone, they cannot accept their way back.

06 · Keep the learning

07 / 07

Make decisions visible.
Make the brief testable.

The product breaks the quiet loop for responders. The skills help the team avoid recreating an unowned, unmeasured fix in the next cycle.

Before work starts

review-checklist

Reviews only the brief's core decision quality. It asks whether someone owns the work, success can be measured, scope stays consistent, and the problem comes before the proposed fix.

  • Catch ownerless work before it enters delivery.
  • Require an observable outcome, not just a list of hopes.
  • Surface scope drift while the change is still a proposal.
After the meeting

meeting-recap

Turns notes into a concise record of what was actually decided and what someone agreed to do.

  • Separates agreement from options and discussion.
  • Records the named owner and due date when notes state them.
  • Marks missing details instead of assigning them by guess.
